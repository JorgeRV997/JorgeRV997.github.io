Fungal biotransformations
& biocatalysis

Fungal whole-cell systems can perform selective chemical transformations under mild conditions. Explore the scientific basis for marine fungal biocatalysis, followed by two published experimental studies on sulfur-containing heterocycles, indanes and phenylpropyl derivatives.

Illustration of a fungal fermentation flask

From fungal culture
to selective transformations

Biocatalysis employs enzymes or whole-cell systems to modify organic molecules, often enabling regio- and stereoselective oxidations, reductions, hydroxylations and hydrolyses. Compared with conventional synthetic routes, fungal transformations can provide complementary reactivity under relatively mild conditions; the resulting products must still be independently isolated and structurally characterised.

The experimental studies below compare Emericellopsis maritima BC17 and Purpureocillium lilacinum BC17-2, two marine-derived strains isolated from Cádiz Bay sediments. Botrytis cinerea UCA992 was also examined in selected phenylpropyl transformations. Structural outcomes and biological activities are distinguished throughout.

LITERATURE REVIEW / 2023 · SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

Why marine-derived fungi as biocatalysts?

Read our review · Frontiers in Microbiology (2023) ↗

Marine-derived fungi are a comparatively underexplored reservoir of enzymes capable of modifying both natural products and synthetic compounds. Their ability to catalyse reactions such as oxidation, hydroxylation, reduction and hydrolysis makes them attractive for the preparation of structurally modified and enantiomerically enriched molecules, sometimes under milder conditions than those required for conventional synthesis.

Our peer-reviewed review, Marine-derived fungi as biocatalysts (Frontiers in Microbiology, 2023), critically surveyed research published through September 2022, organising biotransformation studies by substrate class and examining their potential contributions to synthetic chemistry. It also covered biodegradation and bioremediation applications, illustrating the broader enzymatic versatility of marine fungal systems. The review discusses published whole-cell reactions rather than claiming isolation or characterization of the enzymes responsible.

The review provides the literature foundation for the two experimental papers presented below; it is a review of published research, not an experimental report of newly isolated products.

01 / INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF MOLECULAR SCIENCES · 2025

Oxidation and ring-opening of thiochroman derivatives

Two racemic alcohol substrates—thiochroman-4-ol and 6-chlorothiochroman-4-ol—were separately fed to E. maritima BC17 and P. lilacinum BC17-2. The organisms produced oxygenated derivatives through sulfur oxidation and alcohol-to-ketone conversion; the two strains displayed contrasting stereochemical preferences.

WHY INVESTIGATE THIOCHROMANS?

Thiochroman and thiochromanone frameworks are sulfur-containing heterocyclic scaffolds of medicinal-chemistry interest: related derivatives have shown antimicrobial, cytotoxic, antiviral and antileishmanial activities in earlier studies. Fungal transformations of this framework remain comparatively underexplored, motivating the examination of accessible oxidation states and stereoisomers. These are literature precedents, not biological activities demonstrated for the newly obtained products.

MAIN SCIENTIFIC OUTPUTS
  • Ten known products and one newly reported compound: (S)-1-(5-chloro-2-(methylthio)phenyl)propane-1,3-diol (6a).
  • Unusual sulfur heterocycle ring opening by P. lilacinum BC17-2, responsible for the formation of 6a.
  • Strain-dependent enantioselectivity: P. lilacinum yielded anti-(1R,4R)-thiochroman-4-ol 1-oxide (anti-3) with 82.0% ee, whereas E. maritima produced the corresponding sulfoxides with generally lower optical enrichment.
  • Opposite diastereomeric preferences were observed for the 6-chloro sulfoxides: P. lilacinum favoured the syn products, while E. maritima favoured anti-3a (51.0% de).
  • Stereochemical characterisation by 1D/2D NMR, HRMS, Mosher's method, ECD and chiral HPLC.

Biological activity

Antimicrobial activity was assessed for twelve substrate/product compounds against Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 29213, Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 and a clinical isolate of Candida albicans HPM-1922816.

No significant antimicrobial inhibition in the tested panel.

The newly described ring-opened compound 6a was not included in this particular 12-compound assay list. The principal contribution of the study is the fungal reactivity and its stereochemical outcomes.

02 / JOURNAL OF MARINE SCIENCE AND ENGINEERING · 2025

Stereoselective indane and phenylpropyl biotransformations

Five substrates—indene, indanone, 5-chloroindanone and two phenylpropyl acetates—were incubated with marine-derived fungal strains. Selected phenylpropyl substrates were also transformed using B. cinerea UCA992, broadening the comparative biocatalytic assessment.

MAIN SCIENTIFIC OUTPUTS
  • Sixteen known derivatives isolated, exhibiting variations in enantio- and diastereoselectivity depending on the substrate and fungal strain.
  • 98.2% ee and 100% de for syn-(1S,2R)-indane-1,2-diol (7), obtained from indene with P. lilacinum BC17-2.
  • Additional P. lilacinum products included (R)-3-hydroxyindanone (6) at 71.9% ee and (R)-2-hydroxyindanone (5) at 43.0% ee.
  • From the chlorinated phenylpropyl acetate, P. lilacinum produced threo-(1S,2S)- and erythro-(1S,2R)-diols with 88.5% and 91.2% ee, respectively, illustrating that selectivity extends beyond the indane family.
  • Absolute configuration assignments supported by experimental ECD and calculated ECD/ORD for selected optically active derivatives.
PHARMACEUTICAL RELEVANCEChiral building block for indinavir (Crixivan®)
98.2%enantiomeric excess
100%diastereomeric excess

The syn-(1S,2R)-indane-1,2-diol obtained by biotransformation of indene with P. lilacinum BC17-2 is identified in the publication as a key chiral intermediate for the synthesis of indinavir, an HIV protease inhibitor. This highlights the potential synthetic value of the observed stereoselectivity. The study produced the intermediate, not indinavir itself.

Biological activity

A panel of eight non-chlorinated biotransformation products was evaluated against the phytopathogenic fungus Botrytis cinerea UCA992 by a resazurin-based microdilution assay.

No significant antifungal inhibition was observed under the reported assay conditions.

Related chlorinated compounds reported elsewhere showed different behaviour; those prior findings must not be assigned to the non-chlorinated products tested here.

Scientific perspective

The 2023 review establishes the broader chemical and biotechnological rationale for marine fungal catalysis. The 2025 studies then demonstrate reaction diversity, strain-dependent stereoselectivity and synthetic relevance in defined substrate families, including the production of a chiral indane building block associated with indinavir synthesis. Biological assays are reported separately and without attributing activity to compounds for which none was observed.
