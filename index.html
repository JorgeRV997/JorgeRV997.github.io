RESEARCH CONTEXT

From fungal metabolism to the chemistry of grapevine diseases

Grapevine trunk diseases constitute a complex phytopathological problem involving fungi capable of colonising woody tissues. Understanding the chemistry associated with these organisms can provide insight into their secondary metabolism and help identify metabolites worthy of further investigation.

Within METAFUNVID, my contribution focuses on the chemical and analytical side of this challenge: exploring fungal metabolic profiles, examining complex extracts and supporting the structural characterisation of relevant compounds. This work connects natural products chemistry with phytopathology, without presupposing the identity, biological role or activity of metabolites that are still under study.

02 / SCIENTIFIC REVIEW

Feature-based molecular networking in fungal natural product discovery

A literature-based assessment of how feature-based molecular networking (FBMN), LC-HRMS/MS and complementary annotation tools contribute to the discovery, dereplication and prioritisation of fungal natural products.

The review examines representative published workflows, the strength of metabolite annotations and the opportunities and limitations of molecular networking as a discovery strategy. Annotation is distinguished from definitive structure elucidation.

Contignasterines, Anti-Inflammatory 2-Aminoimidazole Steroids from the Sponge Neopetrosia cf. rava Collected in the Bismarck Sea

This collaborative study examined a Bismarck Sea specimen of the sponge Neopetrosia cf. rava, yielding two new steroids, contignasterines A and B, alongside the previously known contignasterol. Their structural characterization revealed unusual oxidized steroid frameworks bearing a 2-aminoimidazole substituent; contignasterine A also contains a distinctive phosphate group. In cellular experiments using stimulated macrophages, the three compounds reduced the production of pro-inflammatory mediators, including nitric oxide and reactive oxygen species. The two new steroids differ at the C/D ring junction. Contignasterine A gave a negative Cotton effect at 302 nm, consistent with a cis junction. The positive signal at 289 nm for contignasterine B supported a trans junction.

Stereochemical Characterization of Optically Active Indane and Phenylpropyl Derivatives Obtained Through Biotransformation by the Marine-Derived Fungi Emericellopsis maritima BC17 and Purpureocillium lilacinum BC17-2

Marine-derived Emericellopsis maritima BC17 and Purpureocillium lilacinum BC17-2 were investigated as biocatalysts for indene, indanone and phenylpropyl substrates, yielding 16 previously known derivatives. Oxidation, reduction and hydrolysis generated several optically active products, with particularly noteworthy stereoselectivity for P. lilacinum. Electronic circular dichroism (ECD) and optical rotatory dispersion (ORD) calculations supported absolute-configuration assignments, including that of an enantiomerically pure indane diol relevant to organic synthesis. Selected compounds were also tested against the phytopathogen Botrytis cinerea, but showed no significant inhibition under the conditions examined. For the chlorinated indanediol anti-7a, chiral chromatography gave 85.1% enantiomeric excess and more than 99% diastereomeric excess. Calculated optical rotatory dispersion supported the (1R,2R) assignment.

Int. J. Mol. Sci. · 2025 · VOL. 26 · ISSUE 3

Biotransformation of Thiochroman Derivatives Using Marine-Derived Fungi: Isolation, Characterization, and Antimicrobial Activity

The biocatalytic potential of two Bay of Cádiz marine-derived fungi was explored using thiochroman-4-ol and its chlorinated analogue as substrates. Their fermentations yielded ten known derivatives and a previously undescribed ring-opened diol produced by Purpureocillium lilacinum BC17-2. Spectroscopic analyses, chiral chromatography and stereochemical approaches including ECD and Mosher-type derivatization helped distinguish sulfoxide and sulfone products and their configurations. The fungi displayed contrasting stereoselectivities, illustrating how the choice of microbial catalyst affects the product profile. The compounds tested did not exhibit significant antimicrobial activity against the selected human pathogens.

Front. Mar. Sci. · 2024 · VOL. 11

Discovery of New Eremophilanes from the Marine-Derived Fungus Emericellopsis maritima BC17 by Culture Conditions Changes: Evaluation of Cytotoxic and Antimicrobial Activities

This work extended the chemical investigation of Emericellopsis maritima BC17 from solid to liquid cultivation using an OSMAC strategy. Growth in Czapek-Dox and potato dextrose broth afforded three new eremophilane sesquiterpenes and 14 known derivatives, with the new compounds specifically recovered from Czapek-Dox cultures. Their structures and stereochemistry were established using NMR, high-resolution mass spectrometry and ECD. Biological assays identified PR toxin 3-deacetyl as cytotoxic toward five human cancer cell lines (IC₅₀ 2.5–14.7 µM), while selective antibacterial activity was observed only at a high tested concentration. One of the three new eremophilanes, 4-epiisopetasone, belonged to a different stereochemical series from those previously isolated from this strain. Experimental and calculated ECD supported the 4S,5R assignment.

Mar. Drugs · 2023 · VOL. 21 · ISSUE 12

New Eremophilane-Type Sesquiterpenes from the Marine Sediment-Derived Fungus Emericellopsis maritima BC17 and Their Cytotoxic and Antimicrobial Activities

Marine sediment from the Bay of Cádiz provided the fungal isolate Emericellopsis maritima BC17, whose secondary metabolism was explored across different solid media using OSMAC cultivation. Bioassay-guided isolation and spectroscopic characterization revealed four new eremophilane sesquiterpenes, 13 known eremophilane derivatives and two known diketopiperazines. NMR, HRMS and ECD-based analysis contributed to the structural and stereochemical assignments. Among the tested metabolites, PR toxin showed cytotoxic effects against five human cancer cell lines (IC₅₀ 3.75–33.44 µM); (+)-aristolochene also displayed selective antifungal activity, albeit at a relatively high concentration. The results established this marine strain as a chemically productive fungal source.

Marine-Derived Fungi as Biocatalysts

This review critically examines the use of marine-derived fungi as biocatalysts for the transformation of natural and synthetic organic molecules, covering literature published through September 2022. The discussion is organized by substrate class and highlights enzymatic transformations including hydroxylation, oxidation, reduction and other structure-selective reactions. It also considers fungal biodegradation of pollutants and the potential role of marine enzymes in more sustainable chemical processes. Rather than reporting new experimental compounds, the article brings together dispersed biotransformation studies and identifies opportunities and knowledge gaps for future biocatalytic applications.

Molecules · 2022 · VOL. 27 · ISSUE 19

Kribbellichelins A and B, Two New Antibiotics from Kribbella sp. CA-293567 with Activity against Several Human Pathogens

Bioprospecting of saline soils associated with the plant Limonium majus yielded a collection of actinomycetes that was screened under varied cultivation conditions for antimicrobial metabolites. Bioassay-guided purification of active Kribbella sp. CA-293567 extracts led to the discovery of kribbellichelins A and B alongside three known compounds. HRMS/MS, NMR and Marfey’s analysis were used for structure and stereochemical characterization. Both new metabolites inhibited Candida albicans and showed weaker activity against selected Gram-negative bacteria; the study also documented antifungal activity for the known antibiotic sandramycin. These findings illustrate the value of OSMAC and guided isolation in antibiotic discovery.

PROFESSIONAL PROFILE

Academic summary

I am a postdoctoral researcher with a PhD in Biomolecules and Biomedicine with an interdisciplinary background in biotechnology, organic chemistry and the study of microbial natural products. My main strength is the ability to connect microbiological, analytical and chemical approaches within a single research workflow, from the preparation and investigation of biological material to the characterisation of complex organic compounds.

My training has given me hands-on experience across the experimental research process, including the design and implementation of laboratory experiments, microbial cultivation, extraction and purification, instrumental analysis, interpretation of chemical data and assessment of biological activity. I am particularly interested in questions that require combining experimental evidence with computational tools to resolve chemical structures and stereochemistry.

Alongside laboratory research, I have developed experience in scientific writing, the communication of results, collaborative project work and university teaching. I value methodological rigour, reproducibility and the critical integration of different types of data, and I am keen to apply these skills in interdisciplinary academic environments.
