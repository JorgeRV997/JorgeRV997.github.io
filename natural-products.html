RESEARCH CONTEXT

From microbial cultures to characterised metabolites

Microorganisms are important sources of chemically diverse secondary metabolites. My work examines how cultivation conditions influence metabolite production and how complementary chemical and biological techniques can be combined to select, isolate and investigate compounds from complex microbial extracts.

The research workflow integrates the one strain–many compounds (OSMAC) approach with extraction and fractionation, HPLC purification, LC-HRMS/MS and NMR analysis. Depending on the study, metabolomic methods and biological assays help guide compound prioritisation and connect chemical profiles with potential biological relevance.

Two complementary studies of Emericellopsis maritima BC17 under different cultivation conditions.

01 / MARINE DRUGS · 2023

Secondary metabolites from solid cultures

Emericellopsis maritima BC17 was grown on malt agar, potato dextrose agar and rice-based media, following an OSMAC strategy. Malt agar gave the greatest chemical diversity. Isolation and purification were supported by chromatography and HPLC; structures were determined using HRMS, 1D/2D NMR and ECD calculations for stereochemical assignment.

MAIN SCIENTIFIC OUTPUTS
  • Four new eremophilane-type sesquiterpenes, plus 13 known derivatives and two diketopiperazines.
  • Evidence that solid-medium composition substantially changes the fungal secondary-metabolite profile.
  • 13 eremophilanes tested for cytotoxicity and antimicrobial activity.

Biological activity

Cell-viability assays were performed against five human cancer cell lines (HepG2, MCF-7, A549, A2058 and Mia PaCa-2). The previously known PR toxin (compound 16) was the most active eremophilane in this panel.

IC50 3.75–33.44 µMPR toxin (16), across the five tested tumour cell lines; strongest response in A2058 (3.75 µM).

Antimicrobial susceptibility was examined against six bacterial pathogens and two fungi. (+)-Aristolochene (10) showed inhibition of Candida albicans and Aspergillus fumigatus only at the high tested concentration of 471 µM; the remaining tested compounds showed no relevant inhibition in this panel.

The activity results relate mainly to known metabolites; they should not be attributed to the four newly discovered compounds.

02 / FRONTIERS IN MARINE SCIENCE · 2024

Culture-dependent chemical diversity

This follow-up study investigated E. maritima BC17 in Czapek–Dox and potato dextrose broth (PDB) liquid media. Culture conditions determined which eremophilanes could be recovered: the three new compounds were obtained from surface cultures in Czapek–Dox medium. Their structures and absolute configurations were established using HRMS, NMR, NOESY and ECD/TD-DFT.

MAIN SCIENTIFIC OUTPUTS
  • Three new eremophilanes and 14 known derivatives isolated and identified.
  • 4-Epiisopetasone (compound 3) belongs to a different stereochemical series from the eremophilanes previously reported for this strain.
  • A specific set of liquid culture conditions revealed metabolites not encountered in the earlier solid-medium study.

Biological activity

Compounds 1, 3, 11 and 15 were evaluated for antimicrobial and cytotoxic effects. The known PR toxin 3-deacetyl (compound 15) exhibited cytotoxicity in the same five human cancer cell lines.

IC50 2.5–14.7 µMPR toxin 3-deacetyl (15); lowest value in Mia PaCa-2 (2.5 µM).

Compound 15 also exhibited limited activity against methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus at the highest tested concentration (128 µg/mL). The paper relates the observed cytotoxicity of PR toxin and its 3-deacetyl derivative to the aldehyde group at C-12.

The strongest biological activity was associated with a previously known eremophilane, not the three newly discovered compounds.

03 / MOLECULES · 2022 · FUNDACIÓN MEDINA

Kribbellichelins A and B

Bioassay-guided isolation from the halophilic actinobacterium Kribbella sp. CA-293567 yielded two new peptide-like natural products, alongside the known metabolites sandramycin, coproporphyrin III and kribelloside C. Their structures were elucidated through ESI-qTOF-MS/MS, 1D/2D NMR and Marfey’s analysis to assign the amino-acid configurations.

MAIN SCIENTIFIC OUTPUTS
  • Isolation of kribbellichelins A and B following OSMAC screening and active-extract fractionation.
  • Fungal and bacterial pathogen panel with cytotoxicity evaluation in HepG2 cells.
  • First report of antifungal activity for the co-isolated known compound sandramycin.

Biological activity

Both kribbellichelins inhibited the yeast Candida albicans, with compound B being more potent.

A: 15.2 µM · B: 4.1 µMIC50 against C. albicans (11.7 and 3.2 µg/mL, respectively).

They also showed weak inhibition of Acinetobacter baumannii and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and a non-monotonic inhibition profile against Aspergillus fumigatus. No activity against MRSA or substantial HepG2 cytotoxicity was observed at the concentrations relevant to the antimicrobial response.

COMPLEMENTARY APPROACH / METABOLOMICS

Exploring chemical profiles beyond isolated compounds

LC-HRMS/MS-based metabolomics and feature-based molecular networking enable comparisons across cultivation conditions and provide complementary evidence for the prioritisation and annotation of molecular families. This methodological line is relevant to the investigation of the marine-derived fungus Purpureocillium lilacinum BC17-2.

In the doctoral research, leucinostatin-type peptaibols were discussed as putatively annotated metabolites based on molecular-networking evidence. This annotation is distinct from the isolation and complete structure determination of new compounds.

Scope note · The metabolomic annotations presented here should not be interpreted as newly isolated leucinostatins. Further study-specific figures can be added after reviewing the supporting datasets.

Research perspective

These studies illustrate how cultivation strategies, bioassay-guided fractionation, spectroscopic structure elucidation and carefully interpreted in vitro assays complement one another in microbial natural product research. The quantitative results above refer to the test systems and concentrations reported in the original publications; they do not imply validated clinical efficacy.
